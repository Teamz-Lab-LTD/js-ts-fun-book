Pet Insurance: How Much It Really Costs

Real US averages from the NAPHIA industry report — plus what actually moves your quote

Open interactive version (quiz + challenge)

Real-world analogy

A pet insurance premium is like a gym membership priced on your body, your neighborhood, and your history. Everyone sees a different sticker. But there is an industry-wide average, published every year by NAPHIA, and it is a useful sanity check on any quote you get.

What is it?

Cost is set by species, breed, age, ZIP, and the plan levers you pick. According to the NAPHIA 2024 State of the Industry Report, the US average annual accident-and-illness premium in 2023 was $676 for dogs (about $56/month) and $383 for cats (about $32/month). Accident-only ran much lower at roughly $204/year for dogs and $122/year for cats. Your specific quote will land above or below the average based on breed and ZIP.

Real-world relevance

The single biggest reason two owners get very different prices is breed. A 5-year-old Chihuahua in Ohio might quote at $30/month; a same-age English Bulldog in California might quote at $110/month for the same coverage. Same product, same insurer, wildly different base risk. Age is the second lever: enrolling a 10-year-old dog often costs 2–3x what enrolling a puppy would have cost, and some insurers cap new enrollments at ages 12–14.

Key points

  • US average accident-and-illness premium (NAPHIA 2024): $676/yr dog, $383/yr cat — This is the average across all breeds, ages, and states in North America for 2023 data. Use it as a sanity check: a quote 3x this number for a healthy young pet is unusual and worth double-checking against a competitor.
  • Accident-only is cheap (~$200/yr dog, ~$122/yr cat) — but rarely worth it — The NAPHIA 2024 report shows accident-only averages roughly a third of accident-and-illness pricing. The saving is real but so is the coverage gap: cancer, allergies, kidney disease, and every chronic condition are excluded.
  • Breed can double or triple your premium — Breeds with known genetic risks — English Bulldogs, French Bulldogs, Great Danes, Bernese Mountain Dogs, Golden Retrievers — are priced significantly above the average. Mixed breeds usually price at or below average.
  • ZIP code sets local vet-cost expectations — Insurers pull local vet cost data by ZIP. Coastal metros (SF, NYC, LA, Boston) price higher than rural areas because a surgery in San Francisco really does cost more than the same surgery in rural Alabama.
  • ⚠️ Common misconception: 'The premium quoted today is what I will pay forever' — It is not. Premiums re-rate as pets age. Many owners see 8–15% annual increases even without claims. The NAIC Model Act requires this to be disclosed. Budget for a rising line item, especially in years 8–14 of a dog's life.
  • Discounts exist but the base risk drives the price — Multi-pet, annual pay, spay/neuter, and employer group discounts are common — each usually 5–10%. Combined they might trim 15–25% off. That is meaningful but does not overturn the breed and age math.

Line-by-line walkthrough

  1. 1. The table gives you a direct anchor: what the NAPHIA 2024 report says the US average dog and cat policy actually cost in 2023.
  2. 2. Accident + illness is roughly $56/mo dog and $32/mo cat.
  3. 3. Accident-only comes in at roughly a third of that — but you saw in Lesson 4 that it excludes illness and cancer.
  4. 4. The 'what this does NOT tell you' line is the honest limit: your own quote will vary by ±50–100% around the average based on breed, age, and ZIP.
  5. 5. Use the number the way a home buyer uses a Zestimate — a sanity check for wildly off quotes, not a promise of your specific price.
  6. 6. TAKEAWAY: If a quote comes back 3–4x the NAPHIA average, get a second quote before assuming it is normal for your breed.

Spot the bug

You get one quote at $130/month for a 4-year-old mixed-breed medium dog. The salesperson says it is 'normal for this area.'
Need a hint?
Compare against the NAPHIA average and consider what 'normal for this area' would actually justify.
Show answer
The NAPHIA 2024 US average is ~$56/month for dogs. $130/month is more than double the average. A mixed-breed 4-year-old should generally price at or below the average, not above it. Either (a) the levers are unusual — very low deductible, very high reimbursement, unlimited max, plus a wellness add-on — or (b) the quote is high for this carrier and a competitor will beat it materially. Either way, get one more quote before signing. 'Normal for this area' is not a diagnostic; the actual breed, ZIP and levers are.

Explain like I'm 5

On average in the United States, dog insurance costs about $56 a month and cat insurance costs about $32 a month. But your price will be different because it depends on the type of dog or cat you have, how old they are, and where you live. Big dogs, old dogs, and pets in big cities usually cost more. A puppy from a small mixed breed in a small town will pay less than the average.

Fun fact

NAPHIA (the North American Pet Health Insurance Association) publishes its State of the Industry Report every year. It is the most-cited data source in the industry because it aggregates real premium and policy counts from member insurers. Any pet insurance article citing a US average premium is almost certainly citing this report — including this lesson.

Hands-on challenge

Get one real quote for your pet at the standard levers (~$500 deductible, 80% reimb, $10k annual max). Compare to the NAPHIA average for the species. If your quote is more than 2x the average, either (a) your breed/ZIP explains it — verify with a second insurer, or (b) shop again with a different carrier. If it is well under half, double-check the plan levers — you may have a very high deductible masking the price.
