Pet Insurance Plans: Accident-Only vs Accident+Illness vs Wellness

Three plan types, three price points — and the one most people actually need

Open interactive version (quiz + challenge)

Real-world analogy

Think of pet insurance plans like restaurant menus. Accident-only is the kids' menu — cheap, tiny, only covers the basics. Accident + illness is the regular menu — what almost everyone actually orders. Wellness is the drink package — nice to have, but does not save you in an emergency. Mixing them up is where owners overspend.

What is it?

Pet insurance is sold in three plan types. Accident-only pays if your pet is hurt (car accident, bite, cut, swallowed object). Accident and illness — the industry standard — adds coverage for diseases (cancer, allergies, infections, chronic conditions). Wellness plans are optional add-ons that reimburse routine preventative care: vaccines, checkups, dental cleanings. Wellness is not insurance in the classical sense; it is a prepaid maintenance package.

Real-world relevance

The NAPHIA 2024 report shows accident-and-illness policies dominate the US market at over 98% of in-force policies. Accident-only is a small residual for older pets who cannot get full coverage. Wellness is offered as an add-on by most major insurers but sold to a minority of policyholders. In practice, almost every reasonable buyer is choosing between accident-and-illness plans; the debate is just deductible and reimbursement level.

Key points

  • Accident-only is the cheap floor — and rarely worth it — Accident-only plans exist mainly for pets too old for full coverage. They pay for injuries but not illness. Since roughly 70–80% of adult pet claims are illness (allergies, cancer, GI, endocrine), accident-only leaves the biggest bills uncovered.
  • Accident and illness is 'the standard plan' — This is what almost every buyer means when they say 'pet insurance.' It pays for the entire range of unexpected medical costs. Every serious quote should be for an accident-and-illness plan; the only variables are deductible, reimbursement %, and annual max.
  • Wellness is a budgeting tool, not insurance — A wellness add-on refunds you for planned expenses — annual exam, vaccines, heartworm test, dental cleaning. Mathematically it is close to a wash: you pay the insurer $20/month and get back roughly the same in vet visits. Only useful if you struggle to save for those planned bills.
  • Wellness rarely covers what you think — Most wellness plans exclude spay/neuter unless a specific tier is purchased; exclude dental cleanings unless a specific tier; and cap each reimbursable service. Reading the schedule of benefits usually reveals it is closer to a coupon book than a plan.
  • ⚠️ Common misconception: 'Wellness means my whole pet is more covered' — Wellness only adds routine care. It does NOT increase your illness coverage, extend your annual max on the main policy, or lower your deductible. Adding wellness to an accident-only plan does not turn it into an accident-and-illness plan — the illness gap is still there.
  • Match the plan type to the age of the pet you actually have — Young/healthy pet: full accident-and-illness plan is the highest-value purchase. Older uninsurable pet: accident-only is sometimes the only option available. Wellness is optional at any age and should be decided last, on math, not on marketing.

PLAN TYPE COMPARISON (typical US market)
=========================================

                     Accident-Only  A+I (Standard)  Wellness Add-On
Covers injuries          YES            YES              NO
Covers illness           NO             YES              NO
Covers cancer            NO             YES              NO
Covers routine exam      NO             NO               YES
Covers vaccines          NO             NO               YES (capped)
Covers dental cleaning   NO             sometimes        YES (capped)

Who it fits           Old, uninsurable  Almost everyone  Budgeters, kittens/puppies

Rule of thumb: if you can only pick one, pick Accident + Illness.
Wellness is optional. Accident-only is the fallback, not the goal.

Line-by-line walkthrough

  1. 1. The table lines up the three plan types against the questions people actually ask a quote page.
  2. 2. Accident-only sits in one column: it says NO to almost every real-world medical bill except a broken bone or bite wound.
  3. 3. Accident + Illness (the standard) says YES to injuries, illness, and cancer. It is what almost every serious pet insurance policy in the US actually is.
  4. 4. Wellness is a fourth column that only lights up on routine care — vaccines, exams, dental cleanings — and often has small caps on each one.
  5. 5. Pricing rows anchor the trade-off: accident-only is cheap because it covers almost nothing, wellness is cheap because it is capped, and A+I costs the most because it is the only one that pays a real emergency bill.
  6. 6. The last row (who it fits) is the honest summary: almost every buyer should be shopping A+I plans, using deductible and reimbursement to tune the price.

Spot the bug

A salesperson pitches: 'Add our wellness plan and you will be fully covered — no gaps at all.'
Need a hint?
Which specific gaps does a wellness add-on close, and which does it leave wide open?
Show answer
Wellness closes the routine-care gap: vaccines, exams, sometimes dental cleanings and heartworm tests. It does NOT close the illness gap — if the underlying policy is accident-only, chronic disease and cancer are still uncovered. It does NOT raise the annual max, lower the deductible, or shorten waiting periods. 'Fully covered' is not what the wellness product does. The phrase is a sales exaggeration; the policy document is the truth.

Explain like I'm 5

There are three kinds of pet insurance. The first only pays if your pet gets hurt in an accident. The second — the normal one — pays if your pet gets hurt OR sick. The third one is a package that pays you back for the regular vet visits like shots and check-ups. Almost everyone should get the second kind. The third one is like a savings jar with extra steps.

Fun fact

In the NAPHIA 2024 report, over 98% of North American in-force pet insurance policies were accident-and-illness. Fewer than 2% were accident-only. The industry has effectively converged on 'A+I' as the default product — a rare piece of clarity in insurance shopping.

Hands-on challenge

Take one insurer's website and price the same 3-year-old dog on all three plan types side by side. Which plan costs the most per month? Which covers the widest range of vet bills? Compare the monthly delta against the biggest claim each plan would actually pay. The math almost always points the same way — write down which one.
